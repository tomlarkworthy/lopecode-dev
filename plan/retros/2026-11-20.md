A cell's inputs are one line, so any two patches that add an input to the same cell clash. `deployerOps` is the cell most deployer patches change. With `fb-review-key-changed-method-allow` the 3-way merge is clean and `git apply` refuses (`:311`, `:305`): context lines both change.

**Why the system allowed it.** `deploy.ts` checks a passed patch of the owner's four against what runs before it asks him (`already`, `stale`, `moved`; the 11-07 retro's change, after four of six deployer patches were refused at his deploy on 11-06). It does not check it against the patches already waiting for him, which he will deploy first. The drain repairs after the fact: 1800 s after his deploy moves a Worker, `owner-base-moved` hands the others back (`drain2.ts` `baseMovedBack`). The record has 24 such notes on 20 issues since 11-07, 14 of them handing an issue back. By a regex over the workers' next `deploy.ts` comments (rough), 9 of those issues then needed a rebase, and most were handed to him again. Nothing tells him, before he starts, which of the waiting patches go on together.

**Was "working" checked against the live system?** Each patch was checked against what runs, alone. None was checked against the queue it joined.

**Would it have been caught without this run?** At his deploy: the third or fourth `deploy-deployer.ts --patch` of his sitting ends with "the patch does not go on what the Brain runs", and the issue waits for the drain's hand-back, a rebase, a review and his next sitting.

**How others do it.** A merge queue tests each change with the changes ahead of it applied, assuming they pass, and retests the ones behind when one fails: Zuul's dependent pipelines ([Zuul, Project Gating](https://docs.openstack.org/infra/zuul/gating.html)), Bors and its "not rocket science rule" ([Cipriani, 2022](https://tylercipriani.com/blog/2022/02/17/its-still-not-rocket-science/)), Trunk's predictive testing ([docs](https://docs.trunk.io/merge-queue/predictive-testing)).
