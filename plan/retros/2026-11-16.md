**The mechanism.** `deploy.ts` holds the finish of such a task with the label `deploy-held`, written only on its non-dry path (`deploy.ts:303-306`): `if (dry) end({ outcome: "owner-review", … }, 0)` comes before the label write. That was harmless while `--dry` told a worker nothing about such a patch. Since `c38cf5c` (11-14 22:32) and `466f5aa` (11-15 12:58, the owner's B), `--dry` of a held patch answers a browser count, and the brief tells a worker that this is the count that stands (`AGENTS.md`: "a worker gets it with `deploy.ts ID --patch SHA256 --dry`"). So a worker that ran `--dry` alone had its count and its `owner-review`, and handed to review with neither `deploy-held` nor `major` on. The tracker refuses the task's finish while either is on; with neither, it finished the issue at the pass. `drain2.ts`'s rule after a pass (`drain2.ts:1748-1758` after this run's change) assumes "`deploy.ts` put `deploy-held` on at owner-review, so the tracker did not finish it at this pass", and puts `needs-worker` on whatever the state. The workflow has no move out of `done` but `revert`, which needs the owner present (`issue.policy`: `reopen` is from `closed` and `rejected` only).

Of the four patches of this kind passed by a review after 466f5aa, two took this path. The other two, `js-first-deploy-wasm-export` and `deployer-unattended-dry-four`, carried `deploy-held` at their pass, went back to their workers and are at `needs-owner` with his commands. The same shape happened on 10-27 to three deployer patches (`i18866-canary-objects`, `fb-deployer-ref-mismatch`, `deployer-docs-dry-hold`).
