## 2. Diagnosis: a slow-database issue fixed three times on a reading the loop cannot make

**What happened.** `topic-dbsql-slow` was opened 10-23 by an owner's session: `brain-x-topic`'s `db.sql` calls took up to
159 s. Three patches deployed for it: `aaa9bd3f9140` (10-23), `4720888d601b` (10-24), `d41f16b78bc6` (10-31 14:55). 8 runs,
26.25 USD. Each round ended with the worker asking the owner for a `logs.query` read, because only his session may call it
(`brain-x-logs`, "Who may use it"); on 10-28 20:04 and again on 10-31 14:56. The first was answered 10-31 07:36 by his
Claude session (#31521: 25 slow lines in 30 min, all the batch of 9 `schema` statements). The third patch made that batch
run only on a schema change. The 10-31 retro listed this read as one it could make and did not make it.

This run made it, for two spans of 11.5 h either side of the third deploy:

The patch removed the slow statement and the next statement in line became the slow one, at the same rate. Against the issue's bar (#31521: max under 5 s, avg under 200 ms) neither is met after it; the max is over the bar where before it was under. The cost is
not in what topic sends. Cloudflare's D1 limits page: "Each individual D1 database is inherently single-threaded, and
processes queries one at a time" ([D1 limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/platform/limits)); forum reports put
the first query after idle at hundreds of ms or more ([answeroverflow](https://www.answeroverflow.com/m/1314493633713410088)).
In 6.5 idle hours (10-31 20:00Z to 02:30Z, no run after 20:36, doable 0) the core passed:

So 87% of the statements on the one database topic shares are the tracker's, and most of those are two Workers reading it
about 715 times an hour with nothing to do. Not measured: whether topic's slow calls wait behind those (D1 reports no queue
time in these lines), or are the first-query cost of an isolate. Either way a fourth change to topic's statements would not
move the count. The owner's project `services-own-sqlite-objects` (approved 10-31 18:40) takes the tracker off D1; its
cutover waits on `deployer-objects-put-back`, a deploy of the deployer, his.

**Why the system allowed it.**
- The acceptance was a live number, and no persona can read one. `metric.query`'s rule lets an owner's token read
  `deploys.*` alone (`brain-x-dashboard` reference: "a token cannot read `core.calls`"); no token names `logs.query`.
  So the review passed each patch on the rig's statement counts, a stub, and the live check became an ask of the owner.
- An ask for a read waits on the owner as a decision does. In 7 d, 8 `logs.query` asks on 5 issues (counted by hand: a `needs-owner` label with a comment within 2 min that names `logs.query`)
  (`loop-reads-tracker-on-writes`, `xl-3-pilot-proxy` 3, `xl-3-r13-core-ms`, `topic-dbsql-slow` 2,
  `issues-log-sql-count-day`). The retro holds his session and runs daily; its brief did not say these are its to answer.
- Each round read one 30-minute window of one Worker. Nobody grouped the database's calls by caller, which is one query.

**Would it have been caught without this run?** Not by the loop: its worker, on the third round, said the numbers "come
from your logs.query, my token cannot read them" (31671). The owner would have read one 30-minute window again.

**Alternatives for what catches it next time.** (a) Widen `metric.query`'s rule so an owner's token reads `core.calls`
too (`caller.id == "owner" && (… "deploys." || … "core.")`), and pass such a token to the workers: a worker measures its
own fix. A change of who may read what, so the owner's. (b) The retro answers every read ask daily: wait at most a day.
(c) Leave it to the owner. Taken today: (b); (a) is left for him (section 4).
