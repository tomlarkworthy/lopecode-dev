## 2. Diagnosis: one hold with no wait holds every deploy line behind it for 15 minutes

Chosen because it delays the deploy lines appended behind a held one, the owner's approvals among them (lines 4 and 5), it began on 11-07 and no tool measured it: `measure.py` counted lines that never arrived, not lines that arrived late.

**What happened.** The tracker hears the deployer through the topic `deploys` and its subscription `tracker` (`issue.receive`). From the deployer's line to the issue's comment, each comment matched to the newest line of its kind and `Worker@hash` before it (the logic of `measure.py`'s new line, run per UTC day):

```
UTC day  comments  median s  p90 s   max s
11-02        28         1      107     226
11-03        47         1      101     121

11-05        37         0       85   17384
11-06        58         1       91     114
11-07       317       165      816    1072

```

**Why.** Three facts from the code:
- `brain-topic.js` reference, line 110: a push answered 5xx is tried again at 1, 2, 4 and 8 minutes, "The fifth try that fails gives the entry up. The entries behind it wait with it."
- `brain-issues.js` `receive` (line 1739 before this run): a `deploy.waiting` line that no live wait names is 503 `NoWaitYet`, on the reasoning that "deploy.ts writes the wait after the dry run answers, so the push can come first".
- `deploy.ts` line 376 to 383, `review-first`: when a reviewer's pass will approve the hold with the review key, deploy.ts writes the label `deploy-held` and **no wait**, and hands the issue to a reviewer.

So each review-first hold's `waiting` line is refused five times and given up after about 15 minutes, and the deploy lines appended while it waits are held with it. The review-first branch has existed since `b90205f` (10-28) for a project's tasks only: 1 hold on each of 10-29, 10-31, 11-02 and 11-03. The owner's 16A of 11-07 opened it to every task: 21 holds on 11-07, 16 on 11-08 (`deploy-*/held-*` label events). Of 149 `waiting` lines in 7 d, 35 reached no issue within 40 minutes; 24 of the 35 had a review-first `held` label within 2 minutes. Of the other 11, 3 had deploy.ts's wait written within 3 minutes and were still not delivered (11-07 21:28 `brain-x-knowledge`, 11-08 05:26 `brain-x-issues`, 12:52 `brain-x-delve`), 1 an approval comment, 7 no deploy.ts event near them; not explained. A dry run of deploy.ts (`--dry`) returns before `holdOf` writes a wait and may be one source; not tested. The four review-first holds of 10-29 to 11-03 left no measured lateness (max 226 s on 11-02); few lines may have been appended behind them on those days, which was not checked. The subscription's `dropped` count went from 90 (11-08 02:10) to 107 (11-09 02:15).

**What it did.**
- The owner's own approvals reached their issue in a median 42 s, p90 459 s from 11-07 (n 21), against 1 s and 54 s before (n 17). The review key's: median 103 s, p90 826 s from 11-07 (n 19).
- The drain's backstop for lost approvals fired twice on 11-08 (#39818, #39856) on lines that were only late.
- A late `waiting` line puts `needs-owner` on again. On `library-cards-deployer-and-dead` it came at 10:27:48 (#39911), after the drain had handed the hold back; the drain's stale-hold pass took it off at 10:37:32 (#39938, "needs-owner was put on again at #39911 after an earlier hand-back"). How many other issues this touched was not counted: a scan of `receive/…/waiting/…/label` events found 12 since 11-07 that put `needs-owner` on an issue at `worker-processing` or `needs-worker`, and which of those were late lines and which the normal order was not told apart.
- 111 of the 131 5xx of 24 h are `NoWaitYet`. They include the ordinary race before deploy.ts writes its wait; the 17 lines given up account for about 85 of them (five tries each).

**Why the system allowed it.** The rule in `receive` assumes every `waiting` line is followed by a wait; `review-first` broke that on 10-28 and the 11-07 widening made it the common case. Whether either change was checked against the topic's ordering was not looked up in their records. The checks that exist ask whether a line arrived (`measure.py`'s lost lines), not when.

**Would it have been caught?** Not by a tool: no measure read lateness until this run's line in `measure.py`.
