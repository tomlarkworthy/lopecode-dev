5aLeads land on the joints, so the art explains the wiring

Brain
1 cell · hp 20 · art brain
The part draws no joint hardware — that is the shipyard's job, and appears only when a neighbour agrees. What the Brain does draw is one orthogonal trace per joint position, running from the body edge to a square via ring around the die: right angles only, no curves, no glow. The centre is deliberately empty: a mark there would read as an input socket, and the Brain has none. Two pin densities carry the chip read — six short dim pins per edge are decorative package pins, while the two full-length bright pads are the actual joints. Because it is the module the whole ship depends on, the die carries a soft bloom that breathes and the via ring crawls slowly, so the eye finds it first on a crowded hull.

Engine
2 cells · hp 50 · art engine-3
Only the top cell mounts. Its two top-edge and two upper-side joints are live; the two lower joints on that same cell and the six around the bell are blanked, because anything bolted there would sit in the exhaust. The blanked positions still exist on the lattice — they read as dim grey so the player can see the part is deliberately one-sided rather than mis-drawn.

5fRadar — 2×3, mounts on its skirt, two outputs
6 cells · 6 live, 14 blanked

Radar
6 cells · hp 25 · art radar
The dome is glass and the skirt is structure, so only the skirt takes joints: four along its bottom edge and one on each side. Its top edge is a cradle struck concentric with the dome but held clear of it, so an even gap runs the whole way round — the dome sits in a socket rather than on a shelf. From that narrow neck the skirt tapers outward and down, arriving flat exactly where the four bottom joints are and reaching full width only at the two side joints.
This is the one part that earns a sweep — it is the sensor, and a slow rotating line is what a player expects. It runs at a third of the Brain's brightness so it reads as ambient, not urgent.
